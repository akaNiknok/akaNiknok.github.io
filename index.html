A judgment call

Who counts as “drifting” depends on what you call normal.

To find reviewers whose scoring had drifted, I measured each one's score distribution against a reference using Jensen-Shannon divergence. In the same message I wrote down the catch: the reference distribution is our choice, not a truth. Drag the reference and watch the flags move.

Reference weights (drag or use arrow keys)

    Synthetic reviewers shaped like the real analysis: a 1 to 5 scale, only reviewers with 30+ reviews, flagged above a divergence of 0.12. The real finding was two reviewers giving top marks by default. The analysis was adopted and automated.

    Mistakes I caught

    I'd rather catch my own mistakes than defend them.

    1. Jan 2026fixed within 24 hours

      A hoarding fix that slowed down the wrong people

      Shipped
      A daily limit on how many tasks one worker could claim from the shared queue, so nobody could sit on work they weren't doing.
      Broke
      Our fastest QA reviewers hit the same limit, and real review work slowed down.
      Fixed
      Replaced one blunt limit with two: a much higher daily ceiling, plus a small cap on how much anyone can hold at once. Hoarding stayed blocked and QA got its speed back.
    2. Jan 2026rolled back

      A dashboard I asked for

      Shipped
      A new filter on a worker dashboard, built at my request.
      Broke
      It let any worker look up any other worker's submission history.
      Fixed
      I asked for an urgent rollback of my own request, on privacy grounds.
